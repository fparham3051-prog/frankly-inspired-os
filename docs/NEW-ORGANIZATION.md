# Setting this system up for another organization

## How copies work

Every organization gets its **own copy**: its own operations app (with Projects & Team and client workspaces) and its own database, so its data is never mixed with anyone else's. All copies run the **same code from this repository**. A fix merged to `main` reaches every copy on its next deploy, and Frankly Inspired's own copy is one of them.

What makes a copy belong to an organization is its **profile**, not the code:

| Setting (Render environment) | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `ORG_CONFIG` | The organization profile as JSON: the same shape as `org.config.json` (names, leader, brand colors and mark, links, areas, offers). Frankly Inspired's copy has no `ORG_CONFIG` and uses `org.config.json`. |
| `DATABASE_URL` | The copy's own Postgres database (see "Where the database lives"). |
| `PUBLIC_URL` | The copy's address, e.g. `https://ops-riverbend.onrender.com/`. |
| `ADMIN_PASSWORD` | A one-time setup password. It turns off once the owner has two-step sign-in. |
| `SESSION_SECRET`, `ADMIN_API_TOKEN` | Generated by Render. Never reuse another copy's values. |
| `STARTER_PACK` (optional) | Checklists and library materials to start with (see "Starter pack"). |

**Branding.** The pages were written for Frankly Inspired. When the profile names another organization, the app serves every page, script, style, email and notification with that organization's name, leader, links, mark and colors. The tests check that no trace of Frankly Inspired remains (`test/brand.js`). With Frankly Inspired's own profile nothing is rewritten: every file is served exactly as it is on disk.

## Where the database lives

Render's free Postgres database is **deleted 30 days after it's created**, so don't use it for a real copy. Options:
- A free plan that doesn't expire elsewhere (Neon, Supabase or Aiven, for example). Fine for a pilot or a demo; check its storage limit and backups.
- A paid Postgres plan (Render's or another provider's), paid for by the organization. Best for anyone relying on it day to day.

Decide this per organization before step 1. Whichever you choose, copy its connection address into `DATABASE_URL`.

## Setting up a copy (about an hour)

1. **Make the database** (above) and copy its connection address.
2. **Write the profile.** Start from `org.config.json`. Change `org` (names, leader, email, region), `brand` (mark letter and colors), `links` (website, booking), `modules` (see below) and `offers`. Squeeze it onto one line with `node -e "console.log(JSON.stringify(require('./my-org.json')))"`.
3. **Create the service.** In Render: **New > Blueprint**, pick this repository, and it reads `render.yaml`. Rename the service (e.g. `ops-riverbend`) and fill in `DATABASE_URL`, `ORG_CONFIG`, `PUBLIC_URL` and `ADMIN_PASSWORD`. Render generates the rest. The free plan sleeps when idle; the $7 Starter plan keeps it awake. Choose with the organization.
4. **Open it and sign in** with the setup password. Today shows **Set up your system**: a checklist that ticks itself as you go:
   - owner account with your own email
   - two-step sign-in
   - inviting the team
   - connecting email (Settings > Connected email walks through the Google steps)
   - loading contacts, clients or donors from a spreadsheet
   - looking over the starter checklists
   - choosing areas
   - nightly backup
5. **Scheduled tasks.** Copy Frankly Inspired's scheduled tasks (morning brief, daily reminders, nightly backup and retention, checklist automation, weekly market scan if used) with the new copy's address and `ADMIN_API_TOKEN`.
6. **Your own address (optional).** Add the domain in Render and a CNAME record at the registrar.

InstitutionalOS (Diagnostics, scores, 90-day plans, client portal) stays one shared service run by Frankly Inspired. A copy whose `institutionalos` area is on connects to it the same way Frankly Inspired's copy does.

## Starter pack

A new copy starts with a library of checklists (lead to first call, running a Diagnostic, month-end, board onboarding and more) and library materials. By default that's the built-in set. To give a new copy **your current, improved versions**:
1. In your own copy, signed in as owner, open `/api/settings/starter-pack`. It downloads `starter-pack.json`, with your checklists (steps, timing, automation) and library entries, minus owners and history.
2. Paste its contents into the new copy's `STARTER_PACK` setting before its first sign-in.

The text is rebranded to the new organization as it loads. Library links still point to your files; share them with the new organization or replace them.

## Demo copy (for showing prospects)

Make a copy as above with a demo profile, then load fictional data:

```
DEMO_PASSWORD=<its ADMIN_PASSWORD> DEMO_TOKEN=<its ADMIN_API_TOKEN> node scripts/demo-seed.js https://<demo-address>
```

It adds five fictional organizations across the pipeline, invoices, delivery sessions and hours, a project with tasks, a client conversation with a decision, before-and-after scores, and a client workspace link to show. It refuses to run on a copy that already has records.

## Keeping copies current

- Every change goes through a pull request with the tests (`npm test` runs the server tests and the copy and branding tests), then merges to `main`.
- Copies made from `render.yaml` deploy automatically from `main`.
- Database changes in `schema.sql` must stay additive (`CREATE ... IF NOT EXISTS`, `ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS`). Each copy applies them when it starts.

## When to move to one shared system

Separate copies are simplest and safest up to roughly ten organizations. Past that, running one shared app for everyone (every record tagged with its organization, one deploy) becomes cheaper. That's a substantial rebuild; plan it when demand is proven.

## Modules

| Module | Consulting practice | Foundation |
|---|---|---|
| `consulting` (offers, engagement letters) | on | off |
| `institutionalos` (scores, 90-day plans, portal) | on: clients | on: grantees (capacity building) |
| `marketScan` (DMV nonprofit research) | on | on: finding grantees |
| `donorAnalysis` (LYBUNT/SYBUNT, retention) | optional | on: the foundation's own donors |
| `givingLandscape`, `fieldIntelligence` | on | on |
| `myDesk` (owner's personal development) | on | per person |
| `grantmaking` (programs, applications, reviews, awards, payments, reports) | optional | on |
| `fundraising` (donors, gifts, pledges, campaigns, thank-you letters) | optional | on |
| `governance` (board roster, meetings and minutes, conflict-of-interest statements, Form 990 checklist, policies) | optional | on |

Areas can also be switched on or off in **Settings > Areas** without editing the file.

## For the First Choice Construction foundation (grants and fundraising)

What already maps over:
- **Grantees** are InstitutionalOS organizations: intake, evidence-based scores,
  a 90-day plan, a portal, and a funder report the foundation reads.
- **The foundation's donors and gifts** fit the pipeline (prospect to gift) and
  Donor Analysis; Money tracks pledges and receipts the way it tracks invoices.
- **Board** members get Board accounts (view only) in the operations app; the
  monthly report is the board pack.
- **Priorities & Vision** and the Weekly Meeting run the foundation's own work.

Built and ready (Grants and Fundraising in the left menu, under Foundation):
- **Grants**: programs with a budget, dates, scoring criteria, and extra
  questions; a public application form at `/apply/<program>` (or enter
  applications by hand); each reviewer scores 1 to 5 per criterion and can
  declare a conflict of interest; approve with an amount, period, payment
  schedule, and interim/final reports; award and decline letters with standard
  conditions (`grantmaking.conditions` in the profile); budget, committed,
  paid, and what's due on the Overview.
- **Fundraising**: donors (with stage, possible ask, and relationship owner),
  gifts with receipt numbers, pledges paid in installments, campaigns and funds
  with goals, conversations and next steps, thank-you letters with IRS receipt
  wording (set `fundraising.ein` once the foundation has its 501(c)(3)
  determination), year-over-year totals, donor retention, "gave last year, not
  yet this year," CSV import from a spreadsheet and CSV export.
- Set `fundraising.fiscalYearStartMonth` if the foundation's year isn't
  January to December.
- First Choice Construction's own gifts to the foundation: record the company
  as a donor (type Company) and use a campaign such as "Corporate match" so they
  are tracked separately from community gifts.
- **Board & Governance**: the board roster with roles and terms; a year of
  meetings planned in one step, each with an agenda, attendance and quorum
  (a majority of voting members unless `governance.quorum` says otherwise),
  conflicts declared, minutes, motions and votes, and printable agenda and
  minutes; calendar files for every meeting; yearly conflict-of-interest
  statements that each member signs from a private link (no account needed);
  a Form 990 checklist with due dates from the fiscal year (the 990 is due the
  15th day of the fifth month after year-end); and a policy register with
  review dates. Set the foundation's own conflict-of-interest wording in
  `governance.coiStatement` once the board adopts its policy.

Still to build: nothing in this list. Accounts-dependent items (e-signature,
online payments, accounting sync) are tracked separately.
