Trend Charts

A simple projection built from the Scorecard history logged so far: a straight line fit through the recent weeks of each number, with a shaded band for how much it has actually varied. Log at least three weeks to see a trend, more weeks make the projection steadier.

Log a few weeks of Scorecard data to see trend charts here.

What this is, and is not

This is plain least squares regression run on the practice's own numbers, the same math behind a trendline in a spreadsheet, dressed up with a variance band. It is enough to answer "am I trending up or down" for one practice's own pipeline. A client-facing fundraising forecast, donor retention curves, gift capacity modeling, campaign readiness scoring, is a materially bigger build with its own data requirements, and per Before Scale's validation gate that is only worth building once real client demand asks for it, not speculatively.

    Gift Analysis Playbook

    apply what you're studying

    The Weekly Gift Ticker isn't just a log of headlines: every gift it captures is a worked example. This is the training system built around that practice: a repeatable way to read a gift, explain it to the right audience, understand the strategy behind it, and recognize the donor psychology driving it. It's meant to grow, since every new Ticker entry is a candidate for a new worked example, a new tactic, or a new psychology illustration below.

    1. How to analyze a gift: the seven-question framework

    Any gift, a $12,000 annual fund check or a $1 billion campus transformation, can be read with the same seven questions. The questions don't change; only the answers get bigger.

    2. Explaining a gift: calibrate to the audience

    The facts of a gift don't change between audiences. What changes is which facts you lead with and the vocabulary you carry them in. Talking process and mechanics to a VP wastes their time; talking pipeline abstractions to a gift officer sounds like you've never worked a prospect. Both scripts below explain the same Knight/Oregon gift.

    3. Strategy and execution: moves management in practice

    Moves management is the governing theory behind every gift in the Ticker: Identification → Qualification → Cultivation → Solicitation → Stewardship. Knowing the five names isn't the skill; the skill is knowing what to actually do at each stage, and how to tell when a prospect has stalled.

    Diagnostic: a cultivation plan with no next move and no date is a stalled prospect, whether anyone has said so out loud or not.

    4. Donor psychology

    Gift structure and language are downstream of donor psychology. Recognizing which motivation is driving a given donor changes how you talk to them, what you propose, and how you steward them afterward, and it's common for more than one to apply at once.

        Doctoral Prep: Ed Policy & Leadership

        theory, program expectations, current landscape

        Built around your own framework bridging Fryer, Ladson-Billings, and the middle tradition: what a doctoral comprehensive exam in Educational Policy and Leadership would expect beyond it, what EdD and PhD programs structurally require (including how to build a nonprofit-leadership minor that no flagship program publishes), and a current, sourced read on the policy landscape across K-12, higher ed, early childhood/AI, and, deepest and closest to your own practice, philanthropy and the nonprofit sector. Researched Sept 29, 2026 across primary sources, court filings, and field publications; every claim below traces to a named source, and genuine gaps are flagged rather than papered over.

        Where your framework already stands

        Your bridge between Fryer and Ladson-Billings, extended through Rucker Johnson's structural causal analysis, QuantCrit, Dee and Penner's and Gershenson's same-race-teacher-effects research, Sean Reardon, Raj Chetty's Opportunity Insights work, and Anthony Bryk's improvement science, is not a private synthesis you'll need to defend against a program's "real" canon. It's structurally the same bridging move the field's own institutional leadership is currently making, visible in AERA's own 2026 presidential theme (Maisha Winn's "Unforgetting Histories, Imagining Futures," pairing historical consciousness with cross-stakeholder inclusion). A doctoral program will ask you to extend it in four directions, not replace it: with the political-science policy-process theories that explain how policy happens rather than what effects it has; with a third axis of capital theory (Coleman vs. Bourdieu) and the lineage extending your Ladson-Billings pole (culturally sustaining pedagogy, DisCrit, intersectionality); with a methods update, since the difference-in-differences techniques underlying causal work like Johnson's have undergone a real overhaul since 2018-2021; and with a nonprofit-leadership canon that does not exist as a named, published minor at any flagship Ed Policy program, meaning you'll likely build it yourself, cross-school, closer to Indiana University's O'Neill School model than to anything advertised in an Ed Policy catalog.

        1. What comps will expect beyond your framework

        Four adjacent bodies of theory your document doesn't yet name, not a competing framework, but the lattice a comps exam will expect your synthesis to sit inside.

        2. EdD vs. PhD, and the multi-year shape

        The two credentials diverge consistently in kind even where specific credit totals vary by institution (figures below synthesized from the University of Vermont's published comparison table).

        EdD (typical)PhD (typical)
        Research-methods creditsFewer (e.g., UVM: 9)More, often split quant/qual (e.g., UVM: 18)
        Comps formatStandardized/take-home, or dissertation-proposal-as-compsIndividualized, tied to a publishable paper
        DissertationApplied "dissertation in practice" / problem of practice1-3 journal-article manuscripts, or traditional monograph
        Career framingAdministration, government, nonprofit leadershipResearch, scholarship, faculty roles

        A realistic multi-year shape, synthesized across UVM, the University of Iowa, Vanderbilt Peabody, and the University of Rochester: Year 1 is foundational methods and core theory, usually gated behind a software-competency requirement (most commonly SPSS, with R/Stata common in practice); Years 1-2 (PhD) or 1-3 (EdD, often part-time) cover specialization coursework and quantitative/qualitative methods sequences; a comprehensive or qualifying exam follows at the end of coursework, and Rochester's is unusually concrete about it: two questions, 12-15 pages each, a ten-day window, graded Pass / Revise-and-Resubmit / Fail, with two fails on either question triggering withdrawal from the program; and a multi-year dissertation phase runs three to five years for the PhD, a similar or compressed span for the EdD. Full comps reading lists were not found publicly posted for any of eight flagship programs searched (Harvard GSE, Stanford GSE, Teachers College, Vanderbilt Peabody PhD, USC Rossier, UCLA, Michigan State, UW-Madison); what's here is inference from published program structure and canonical literature, a distinction worth naming to a committee rather than glossing over.

        3. Building a nonprofit-leadership minor that doesn't formally exist

        None of the eight flagship Ed Policy and Leadership programs searched publish an explicit "nonprofit leadership minor." The clearest, most important finding for your specific situation: this credential is administrative and relational to build, not something you select from a catalog, and it's worth raising directly in admissions conversations rather than assuming from published materials.
