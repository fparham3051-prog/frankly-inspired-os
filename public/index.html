On the login question

This site sits behind a single shared password, entered once per device, so it can be opened from any browser or phone without going through Claude or any other account. The password can be changed at any time. If an associate ever joins and needs their own access instead of the shared one, that is a real login system to add later, not something this version needs yet.

Where the Philanthropic Dollar Goes, Nationally

Giving USA's nine recipient categories for 2025, the most recent year reported. Religion remains the largest single category; education, public-society benefit, and environment/animals organizations grew the fastest year over year.

On these figures

These are Giving USA's own published per-category dollar figures for 2025, not a percentage share applied back to the total. Giving USA compiles its recipient-category estimates from different underlying data than its source-side total, so the categories above do not sum exactly to the confirmed 2025 total of $617.20 billion — a known feature of how the two sides are modeled, not an error here. Gifts to individuals is a real category (Giving USA notes it, mostly in-kind gifts of medication) but no 2025 dollar figure has been published for it, so it is left off these bars. Source: Giving USA 2026, the Giving USA Foundation and the Indiana University Lilly Family School of Philanthropy. See National Giving Trends below for the same categories back to 2000.

Trend Charts

A simple projection built from the Scorecard history logged so far: a straight line fit through the recent weeks of each number, with a shaded band for how much it has actually varied. Log at least three weeks to see a trend, more weeks make the projection steadier.

Log a few weeks of Scorecard data to see trend charts here.

What this is, and is not

This is plain least squares regression run on the practice's own numbers, the same math behind a trendline in a spreadsheet, dressed up with a variance band. It is enough to answer "am I trending up or down" for one practice's own pipeline. A client-facing fundraising forecast, donor retention curves, gift capacity modeling, campaign readiness scoring, is a materially bigger build with its own data requirements, and per Before Scale's validation gate that is only worth building once real client demand asks for it, not speculatively.

How this is built

LYBUNT is a donor who gave in the last complete fiscal year but not yet this year. SYBUNT is a donor who gave in an earlier fiscal year but not last year or this year. Retention rate compares the last two complete fiscal years only, donor count and dollars both, since the current year is still in progress and would understate it. Tier is based on a donor's total giving in the last complete fiscal year, not a single gift. Nothing typed or uploaded here is sent anywhere or saved — the moment you close or refresh this page, it's gone.

Gift Patterns & Portfolio Implications

How this stays current

This list is not pulled live while you browse, a page cannot search the web on its own. Instead, a scheduled research pass runs weekly, reads leading sources such as the Chronicle of Philanthropy, Giving USA, the DAF Research Collaborative, Fidelity Charitable, Candid, Independent Sector, and the Urban Institute, and writes a fresh set of items here, four each for philanthropy, donor-advised funds, fundraising, and the nonprofit sector. Ask in chat any time for a refresh sooner, or for a deeper dive on any item below. Gift Patterns & Portfolio Implications, above, runs on the same weekly cadence right after the Gift Ticker refresh — a standing cross-gift read (where the money's coming from, what it means for reading the next gift, and portfolio implications for a client's own major-gift strategy), replaced each week rather than accumulated.
